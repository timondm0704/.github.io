About Me

I'm a Research Data Analyst at Northeastern University's Bouvé College of Health Sciences, based at the Roux Institute, where I analyze genetic and clinical data in collaboration with MaineHealth. I'm also pursuing a PhD in Northeastern's Industry Interdisciplinary PhD program.

At heart, I've always been a data person. My path has crossed continents and disciplines: GIS in China, then 11 years analyzing power supply data at a major electric utility, then a master's in Applied Data Analytics at Boston University, and now biomedical research. Whatever the domain, the core of the work is the same: building reliable data pipelines, choosing the right statistical approach, and finding the story hidden in large, complex datasets.

Research

Variance heterogeneity (VH): detecting hidden subgroups in populations by finding distribution patterns that standard mean-based methods miss.

Statistical methods for VH: simulation studies comparing multiple methods for detecting VH across different distributions, feeding into BifurcatoR (manuscript in preparation), our team's Shiny app for detecting, modeling, and interpreting VH in biological data.
Gene-by-environment effects in mice: identifying genetic variants that control VH in metabolic traits across 22 chromosome substitution strains under high-fat high-sugar vs. control diets.
Parent-of-origin effects on obesity (first-author project): examining how FTO variants affect offspring BMI differently depending on parental inheritance, using the Framingham Heart Study cohort.
Pharmacogenetics of bone mineral density: genome-wide association analysis of beta-blocker response in a multi-site randomized controlled clinical trial, run on an HPC cluster.
